# King Emeric of Ardeval

Id: `king` · [Name registry](../names.md)

## Role and class

- **Role:** the main antagonist. His motive is **bringing someone back** (Nick,
  gate 1): his son, Prince Aurel.
- **Class:** not a battle unit in Act 1. His class for the final battle is set
  by the Act 3 tickets (a lord-like armoured enemy; possibly no battle at all
  if the finale's last fight is the Door itself).
- **Age:** 58.

## At a glance

A capable, dutiful king for thirty years. He was cold to his court and warm
only to his son. Three years ago he gave the order that ended the Border War:
the Candles would burn Brennmark's army on the Ashfields. His son Aurel, 15,
had ridden into that field against orders, and burned with it. The court was
told Aurel died leading a charge. The king knows exactly whose order killed
him. Since then he has not appeared in public, and has let Keeper Crane teach
him the Door Vow.

## Want, need, flaw, secret

- **Want:** Aurel back, whatever it costs, and it costs lives.
- **Need:** to grieve, to let the dead be dead, and to take the blame for
  what he did instead of making the world pay for it.
- **Flaw:** grief turned entitlement ("I gave this kingdom everything. It owes
  me one life."). He can't bear to be the man who killed his son, so he has
  to be the man who brings him back.
- **Secret:** his own burning order killed Aurel. Nobody alive knows but him,
  Crane and one battle-mage (Oriel Mast, who gave the Candles the order and
  sees Aurel ride in; Chapter 6).
- **Pressure:** the Door needs a war. He is provoking one against Brennmark,
  and Brennmark is starting to believe it.

## Arc

- **Start:** a grieving father hidden inside a king, and the story's unseen
  hand.
- **End:** at the Door, he gets what he wanted, or what's left of it, and
  finally lets go. He shuts the Door himself, or lets the lead do it; he dies
  or abdicates. That's decided by the Act 3 outline and the midpoint twist.
  **Not a tragedy for the player:** his letting go is part of the victory.

## Voice notes

- Formal and measured, with long sentences and royal "we" in public, "I" in
  private. Never raises his voice.
- Speaks of Aurel in the present tense. Gentle only when he does.
- **Won't say:** "dead" about Aurel. He says "away" or "gone ahead".

**Sample lines**

- "My son is away. When he comes home, he will find his kingdom as he left
  it. That is all I ask of it."
- "A war is a terrible thing. We have had one. We know the price. We intend,
  this time, to be paid."
- "I gave the order. Do you understand? I said 'burn it', and he was in it."
