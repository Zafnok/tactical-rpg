# Hollis Marr

Id: `retainer` · [Name registry](../names.md)

## Role and class

- **Role:** the mentor (the old retainer, Nick's pick at gate 1) and **the
  scripted Act 1 loss** (Chapter 8).
- **Class:** **Guard** (armoured, spears). He was captain of House Veyne's
  guard for twenty years.
- **Talent:** HP.
- **Age:** 56.

## At a glance

Hollis followed the lead into exile because he believes a debt is paid in
person. He is gruff and dry, and would rather mend a fence than talk. He's
also Dace's father. He has suspected for years what his son really did before
the night at the mill, and has never said a word about it.

## Want, need, flaw, secret

- **Want:** to keep the lead alive, and to have his son back as he remembers
  him.
- **Need:** to stop protecting Dace from the truth, and to tell it himself.
- **Flaw:** loyalty that becomes silence. He'd rather carry a thing alone than
  say it and break something.
- **Secret:** after the exile, Hollis found a purse of Vosse's silver hidden in
  Dace's old room, and a list of names in Dace's hand. He burned both. He
  let the lead go on believing Dace was a hero, because he couldn't bear to be
  the one who said otherwise.
- **Pressure:** his daughter Wren was tested by Piers and taken by the Vigil
  three years ago, and "died at the Ashfields". He has never forgiven Piers,
  and never forgiven himself for letting her go.

## Arc (start → end: Act 1 only; he dies in Chapter 8)

| Chapters | Where he is |
| -------- | ----------- |
| Ch1 | Recognises his son's handwriting on the letter and his son's seal on the kill order, and says nothing. |
| Ch3 | The Greywater register puts Dace's name among Vosse's paid informants. Hollis goes very quiet; Rue notices. |
| Ch4 | At Veyne Hall, Dace doesn't deny it. The lead learns Hollis *knew*. Their bond cracks. |
| Ch5–7 | He earns it back by telling the truth, all of it, to the lead and to Rue. Side quest "The Captain's Oath". |
| Ch8 | Holds the Saltmere gangway against the Hounds while the party boards. Dace orders the archers to stop, too late. Hollis dies between his son and the lead, telling Dace he's ashamed of him and loves him, both. |

- **Start:** a silent protector of a lie.
- **End:** a father who told the truth and paid for it. His death is where
  Dace's turn begins (it pays off in Act 3).
- **Classic permadeath:** if Hollis falls in battle before Chapter 8, his
  final scene can't play. The Chapter 8 script needs an alternate version in
  which his absence is felt and Dace hears the truth from the lead instead
  (flagged for the Chapter 8 script ticket).

## Voice notes

- Short, flat sentences. Soldier's grammar ("Aye." "Not today." "Eat
  something."). Never swears in front of Piers, and constantly in front of
  Tamsin.
- Calls the lead "Veyne", or "kid" when he's worried, and very rarely by
  first name, when it matters. Never a gendered word ("lad", "lass"): the
  script is gender-neutral (`setting-and-tone.md`).
- Dry humour by understatement. Never makes a speech, except once, in
  Chapter 8.
- **Won't say:** Dace's name, if he can avoid it. He says "the boy" or "him".

**Sample lines**

- "Wooden swords. Four years of wooden swords. Try not to cut yourself on the
  real one."
- "I've buried better men than Harl Coster. I'll dig this one's hole myself,
  it's the least I can do for him."
- "I knew. Not all of it. Enough. I told myself enough wasn't knowing."

## Portrait brief

- **Silhouette:** broad and square, with a thick neck, and the tall collar of
  a mail coif pushed back. A spear haft at the edge of the frame.
- **Hair:** grey, cropped short, receding. A full grey moustache and a
  close-cut beard.
- **Face:** weathered and square-jawed, with a broken nose. Heavier shading
  is fine here: he's 56, and the lines are earned.
- **Clothing:** an old dented breastplate over mail, a faded blue surcoat with
  the Veyne crest still stitched on (he never cut his off), and a worn leather
  gorget.
- **Colours:** steel grey, faded Veyne blue, rust brown and a white moustache.
- **Expressions:** `neutral`, `happy` (a rare, crooked smile), `angry`, `sad`,
  `surprised`, plus `weary` (eyes half shut, for his confessions).
