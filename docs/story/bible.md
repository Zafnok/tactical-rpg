## Premise

Three years ago the kingdom of **Ardeval** won a ten-year war against
**Brennmark** by burning Brennmark's army on the **Ashfields** with vows of
fire. King **Emeric's** only son, Prince **Aurel**, burned with it. The king
never came back from that day. With **Keeper Absalom Crane** of the Vigil, he
is working the one forbidden vow that can open **the Door** of the dead god
Othe and bring his son back. The Door is paid for in lives, and a new war
would pay for it many times over. The lead, heir of **House Veyne**, was
exiled four years ago for a killing their best friend **Dace Marr** committed.
The lead confessed, and a noble's exile was the price of a commoner's rope.
The lead has lived since then in the **Thornmarch**, a poor border country full
of unpaid veterans turned brigand, forbidden to leave it or to use the family
name. The story begins the day someone is sent to kill everyone who was there
that night. The order bears the Veyne seal, which Dace now holds.

## Geography

The home continent is **the Old Kingdoms** (Acts 1 and 3). Across the eastern
sea lies **the Jade Reach** (Act 2), and in the far south **the Sunward
Isles**, which only sailors' stories reach. The Old Kingdoms know both
exist; almost nobody has been to either.

## History

- **The Long Dusk** (about a thousand years ago). The gods died. Nobody agrees
  how. What's left of them is memory and power, and people born gifted can
  swear vows to it. The Vigil was founded to keep their vows and their names.
  The histories are told in fragments: ruins, prayers and item text, never a
  lecture.
- **The Door** (lore, a fragment). Othe the Doorkeeper kept the door between
  the living and the dead. Before the gods died, someone in the East opened it
  once, and the Jade Reach remembers what came through. The Vigil has buried
  the Door Vow ever since.
- **The Border War** (13 to 3 years ago). Ardeval and Brennmark fought over the
  northern passes and the Thornmarch fords. It was ten years of attrition,
  cheap lives and debts.
- **The Candles** (6 years ago). Losing, the crown let the Vigil take gifted
  children young and swear them to fire. Soldiers called them "the Candles".
  The king's man in the Thornmarch, **Harrick Vosse**, gathered children for
  them, and paid locals to point them out.
- **The night at the mill** (4 years ago). Dace Marr, the lead's best friend
  and the son of House Veyne's guard captain, killed Vosse when Vosse took
  Dace's own sister, Wren. Dace begged: "They'll hang me. They won't hang
  you." The lead, 17, confessed. Lord Veyne gave up the house's title to save
  his heir's life, the lead was exiled to the Thornmarch, and Hollis Marr, the
  captain, went too, unasked. A girl named Rue escaped from Vosse's cart that
  night. What the lead doesn't know: Dace had spent a year selling other
  families' gifted children to Vosse.
- **The Ashfields** (3 years ago). The king ordered the Candles to burn
  Brennmark's army on the plain. It ended the war. Prince Aurel, 15, had ridden
  into the field against orders to lead a charge, and died in the fire. The
  official story is that he died leading the charge. The truth, which the king
  knows, is that his own order killed his son. Wren Marr, taken as a Candle a
  year after the mill, was on that field and didn't die there. Her family was
  told she did.
- **The Ash Peace** (3 years ago to now). The treaty ended the war, but the
  army was never paid; the unpaid veterans turned brigand. Dace, a war hero at
  19, was given House Veyne's lands and title and made captain of the king's
  **Hounds**. The king withdrew from court. Crane began to teach him the Door
  Vow.
- **Now.** The Door Vow needs lives given "under the vow": deaths near a
  gifted bearer who has sworn it. A war would supply them. The king is
  provoking one. A routine Vigil report from Harrowby mentioned "a gifted girl
  with burned hands" sheltering at the chapel, and Crane recognised a
  surviving Candle. He ordered everyone from the night at the mill silenced.
  The order went to Dace.

## Themes

1. **What we owe the people we love, against the truth about them.** The lead
   and Dace, Hollis and Dace, the king and Aurel, Dace and Wren: every major
   relationship asks how far love should cover for someone.
2. **Letting go.** The king can't. Dace can't. The lead must let go of who Dace
   was, and the ending is won by characters who learn to. It's a victory, not a
   tragedy, because the living choose each other over the dead.
3. **A rotten system is made of small compromises.** No one in the Candles
   business thinks of themselves as a monster: a keeper writing names in a
   register, a boy paid to point, a king who signs a paper. The party is made
   of people who each made one of those compromises and choose to stop.

## Tone rules

Tone B (`setting-and-tone.md`): dark with warmth and humour, like FE Path of
Radiance, Triangle Strategy, Yakuza and Persona.

- **The humour is in-character**, never the narration winking at the player:
  Tamsin's swagger, Piers's nerves, Rue's bad manners, Aske's deadpan and
  Hollis's dry grumbling. Banter between battles, gallows jokes in them. Later
  recruits can be broader oddballs (Yakuza side-story energy), agreed at gate 1.
- **OK to joke about:** hunger, pay, bad food, the weather, each other,
  bureaucracy, the Vigil's paperwork, nobles' manners, the lead's lost title.
- **Never a joke:** the Candles, the victims of the false-flag massacres,
  grief itself, anyone's dead.
- **How dark it gets:** war crimes and the taking of children are real and
  central, but they happen **off screen** and are shown by their aftermath
  (empty cots, a register, a burned mill), never lingered on. Named characters
  die, including the scripted Act 1 loss (Hollis). Violence is FE-level: blood
  is implied, and there's no gore. **No sexual violence, ever.**
- **No romance or shipping** (Nick, 0007). Relationships are friendship,
  rivalry, mentorship and family. Hollis was married before the story; his
  wife died years ago, and that is the only romance in the backstory.
- **The ending** is a resolved, hard-fought victory, never a tragedy (Nick).
  Losses along the way are allowed and matter.
- **The villains are understandable.** Every villain wants something a player
  could want: a son back, a sister back, pay owed, an end to death. The story
  argues with them; it doesn't sneer at them.
- **The lead** follows the Persona-style rules in `setting-and-tone.md`: few
  spoken lines, reply tones at key moments, no fixed personality. The world
  treats the lead as the exiled heir of Veyne; how the lead feels about that is
  the player's call.

## Rules of magic: vows

Nick picked **vows** at gate 1. Mechanics are in
[`docs/design/magic.md`](../design/magic.md); this is what they *are*.

- **The dead gods.** The gods died in the Long Dusk. Their attention lingers,
  like warmth in an empty bed. The ones whose vows are still sworn:
  - **Ama, the Mother** (mending): healing vows.
  - **Vael, the Pyre** (hearth and pyre): fire vows.
  - **Hrim, the Long Winter** (frost): frost vows.
  - **Orun, the Hand** (force): force vows.
  - **Othe, the Doorkeeper** (death): **forbidden**. His one known vow is the
    Door Vow.

  Other gods' vows are lost, which leaves room for more elements later.
- **The gifted.** About one child in three hundred is born able to swear a
  vow. Nobody can tell until the child is tested, usually by a Vigil keeper.
  The lead is not gifted.
- **A vow is a promise plus a power.** The caster swears a promise to a dead
  god's memory, such as "I will not turn from the wounded" for Ama's Heal, or
  "I will burn only what I must" for Vael's Fire, and the god's leftover
  attention answers. **A spell is a vow.**
- **Uses per battle.** A vow answers a few times, then the god's attention
  drifts. It comes back with rest. This is why every spell refills between
  battles and never mid-battle.
- **Class spells** are the vows a tradition teaches (the Vigil's healing
  forms, the crown's war forms). A vow **kept to its end** is yours for good.
  That's class mastery, and why you keep a mastered class's spells after a
  class change and lose an unmastered one's (`magic.md`).
- **Signature spells** are a vow of your own, sworn in your own words outside
  the Vigil's forms. The Vigil calls that heresy unless it approves the words.
- **The Final Vow.** A caster who goes deep enough into a tradition swears to
  lay down steel for good. That's why tier-3 magic classes carry no weapons,
  healers included.
- **Terrain.** A vow calls a sliver of the god into the world: Vael's fire
  sets a forest alight, and Hrim's frost freezes a river where it lands.
- **Echoes (elementals).** When a caster dies with a vow still burning,
  usually on a battlefield where many did, the vow sometimes goes on without
  them. An echo of Vael is walking fire. It drinks fire because it *is* Vael's
  fire, and Hrim's frost is its death, and the reverse for an echo of Hrim.
  Echoes are uncommon, and the Ashfields are full of them.
- **The Vigil's law.** Every gifted child must be registered. Healing vows are
  the Vigil's; fire, frost and force vows are licensed to the crown's
  battle-mages. Swearing a vow on your own is heresy, and heretics are
  hunted. Breaking a vow's promise is a sin in the Vigil's eyes. **In the game
  a broken promise never removes a spell**: that's story flavour only.
- **The Door Vow** is sworn to Othe. It opens the Door for one soul to come
  back, and its price is lives: every death "under the vow", meaning near a
  gifted bearer who has sworn it, pays toward the opening. The Candles who
  survived the Ashfields are the king's bearers. What exactly comes back
  through the Door is a gate-2 question (the midpoint twist).
- **The East doesn't swear vows.** In the Jade Reach they train **breath**, a
  discipline of body and spirit (the wuxia continent). How that works in play
  is decided when Act 2 is designed.
